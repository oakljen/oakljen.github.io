Selected work

Two products I designed, built and shipped on my own. Both are live — the captures below are the real sites, not mockups.

Fig 01 · filmkit.studio

FilmKit

Unit 01 // SaaS · solo build

Studio management for video production teams who are currently duct-taping Drive, email and spreadsheets together.

Delivery, revisions, client sign-off and invoicing usually live in four different places, and the handoff between them is where a production loses a week. FilmKit puts the whole chain on one job record, so a client's "can we see v3 again?" stops being an archaeology dig through a shared inbox.

Delivery
Versions stay attached to the job instead of the thread they were emailed in.
Sign-off
Approval is a recorded action, not someone saying "yep looks good" on a call.
Invoicing
Billing hangs off the same job record, so it doesn't get re-typed.
Betafilmkit.studio ↗
Fig 02 · trainytracker.com

TrainyTracker

Unit 02 // PWA · realtime

Real-time train tracking across NSW & Victoria, built for the phone that's already in your hand on the platform.

Live positions, platform info and timings, refreshed every 30 seconds across three networks. It's an installable PWA rather than a store app because the moment you need it is the moment you're standing on a cold platform wondering whether the 7:42 exists — that shouldn't require a download.

Positions
Where the train is now, not where the timetable says it should be.
Timings
Platform and departure — the two things you check before you run.
Install
Add to home screen, works like an app, no store listing.

About me

Officially I'm on the support desk. In practice I'm the one people escalate to when something actually needs fixing — and I like fixing things properly the first time.

I work IT and customer support for a nationwide company. Officially I'm on the support desk, but I'm usually the one people escalate to when something actually needs fixing — I've ended up the most knowledgeable person on the team about how the platform works under the hood.

Outside of work, the same problem-solving turns into a hobby — pointed at my own homelab instead of someone else's spreadsheets.

I studied Computer Science at Western Sydney University, and I've spent the years since turning that into practical skills: dashboards that replace hours of manual copy-pasting, networks that don't fall over, and the occasional vintage rotary phone that now runs on SIP.

I like fixing things properly the first time, explaining what I did in plain English, and not disappearing once the invoice is sent.

Common questions

What does it cost?

There's no price list here, because I'd be making it up before I know what the job is. You tell me what's wrong, I diagnose it, and you get scope and cost in plain English before I start. If the answer is "this is a five-minute fix, don't pay me for it," I'll say that too.

Do you work remote or on-site?

Both. I'm in Sydney, so on-site is straightforward around the city. Most reporting and automation work happens remotely anyway.

What happens if it breaks again?

I fix it. That's the short answer. The longer one is that I leave notes on what I changed and I'm still reachable after the invoice is sent — a job isn't finished the moment I get paid.

I don't know what's actually wrong. Is that a problem?

No — that's most of the job. "It's slow", "it keeps doing the thing", or "someone set this up in 2019 and left" are all fine starting points. Working out what's actually happening is the part you're hiring me for.

Are you also looking for full-time work?

I'm currently in IT and customer support at a nationwide company and studying Computer Science at Western Sydney University. If you're reading this page as a hiring manager rather than a client, the same email works.
